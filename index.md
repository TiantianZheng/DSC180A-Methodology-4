## Quarter 2 project brainstorm

**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**  

The most interesting topic to me this quarter has been using natural language processing (NLP) to analyze patient reviews and turn unstructured feedback into meaningful insights. I find it interesting how sentiment analysis and severity classification can help us understand not only whether patients are satisfied, but also how serious their concerns are. Especially in the fertility care industry, where patient experiences can be complex and emotionally sensitive, these techniques can help clinics identify issues that require attention.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**  

For Quarter 2, I would like to investigate how reliable our review monitoring system is when dealing with limited or unevenly distributed review data. Specifically, I am interested in examining whether changes in sentiment and complaint frequency reflect meaningful trends or random fluctuations. I would like to compare different monitoring methods and evaluate their ability to identify important changes while minimizing false alarms.

**What is a potential change you’d make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**  

One change I would make is to place more emphasis on evaluating our dashboard and alert system with real data and user feedback. During Quarter 1, we have focused on building a functional Streamlit prototype and integrating outputs from other tracks. For Quarter 2, I would like to systematically test how useful and reliable the dashboard is for different users, such as marketing staff and executives in two different perspectives, and use their feedback to improve its design and functionality.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**  

I would be interested in exploring statistical change-point detection and time-series analysis to identify meaningful shifts in patient feedback. I would also like to use bootstrapping and sensitivity analysis to evaluate how stable our results are under different sample sizes and alert thresholds. These techniques could help us better understand the uncertainty in our analysis and improve the reliability of our monitoring system.
